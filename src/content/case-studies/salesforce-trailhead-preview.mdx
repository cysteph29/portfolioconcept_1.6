<h3 id="solve-solution">Solve brought immediate answers into Trailhead, grounded in Salesforce documentation with sources users could check.</h3>

Our hypothesis: grounding answers in Salesforce documentation could make Solve more useful for Salesforce work than a general-purpose chatbot.

Users could ask a question, check linked sources, and adjust the answer’s depth to their expertise. For questions needing human input, Solve offered an escalation path: drafting a ready-to-post question for the Trailblazer Community.

## How we got there

<Section id="research" sections={frontmatter.sections}>

## Engagement data showed experienced users were dropping off after certification. Our interviews explored why.

I led recruitment and wrote the interview guide for our team. We explored how experienced users used Trailhead, then examined their behavior around learning and community participation.

Their experiences pointed to faster answers elsewhere, learning that didn’t match their expertise, and the burden of answering repetitive community questions.

<Section id="ideation" sections={frontmatter.sections}>

## We explored whether community participation and visible skills could give experienced users another reason to return.

### An AI persona could answer repetitive questions, but speaking for real people introduced trust concerns.

We explored an AI persona that answered community questions using a member’s previous posts. Letting it speak on their behalf raised authenticity concerns; requiring approval reduced the time it could save. We didn’t pursue the concept.

<div className={openingStyles.placeholder}>
  <p className={openingStyles.placeholderLabel}>
    {"AI-persona concept placeholder: answering on a member’s behalf, with the tradeoff between trust and approval effort."}
  </p>
</div>

### I advocated for a public skill profile, but its value depended on recognition beyond Trailhead.

The profile would showcase certifications, badges, and current skills. Its usefulness depended on employers recognizing it—something we couldn’t control. We set the profile aside but reused its skill-visualization idea in the personalized Trailmixes concept.

## I built a working chatbot so participants could test Solve with their own Salesforce questions.

I built the chatbot with OpenAI and curated Salesforce documentation so participants could ask real work questions and inspect the answers and sources. This let us test the experience without access to Agentforce or Salesforce’s proprietary data.

<Section id="reflection" sections={frontmatter.sections}>

## Building Solve taught me to design when AI should answer, clarify, or offer a path to human help.

Working with a live chatbot surfaced situations I hadn’t accounted for in static screens: misunderstood requests, unsatisfying answers, and conversations that weren’t getting anywhere. I learned to think through how the system could clarify intent, recover from misunderstandings, and offer escalation when another AI response wasn’t helping.

### I can now build and test the interactions I design.

Building the prototype expanded my skills into front-end development, API and tool-call integration, authentication, and basic backend work. I can now take an interaction into working software and use its behavior to inform my next design decision.
