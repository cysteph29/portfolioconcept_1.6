## Project overview

Salesforce Trailhead is Salesforce's learning platform. This 10-month capstone explored how to re-engage experienced users after certification. I led ResearchOps and contributed to strategy and AI prototyping on a five-person team.

<Section id="problem" sections={frontmatter.sections}>

## Trailhead helped professionals learn Salesforce and get certified. But once they were on the job, they looked elsewhere for answers.

<TrailheadLearnerJourney />

We reached 650+ Salesforce professionals, qualified 72, and interviewed 12 across two rounds. Our first questions focused on what would make Trailhead more inspiring and produced agreeable opinions. Looking at behavior revealed experienced professionals turning to ChatGPT, Slack, and YouTube for practical problems instead.

## Solve the work question, then bring learning back into the flow.

Experienced users were leaving Trailhead for practical Salesforce answers, while much of its learning felt too basic for where they were. We addressed both moments: Solve returned sourced answers to Trailhead, and personalized Trailmixes connected their experience to what would help next.

Agentforce and Salesforce's proprietary data were unavailable. We built the prototype with OpenAI and curated Salesforce documentation to test that experience.

### When real-world questions left Trailhead, Solve brought sourced answers back in.

Solve connected a plain-language Salesforce question to a sourced answer and relevant Trailhead learning.

Users could adjust answer depth to their expertise. When Solve fell short, it drafted a question for the Trailblazer Community.

### When learning felt too basic, personalized Trailmixes made the next step relevant.

Personalized Trailmixes used skill-gap insights, experience, career needs, and previous activity to recommend learning without waiting for another question.

The dashboard also surfaced certification-renewal reminders—a supporting usability improvement that helped users keep required credentials current.

Using Figma MCP, we turned design-system components into a working prototype people could question. Twelve research-based AI agents helped catch usability issues before human sessions; they weren't a substitute for user evidence.

## Two promising ideas we explored—and rejected.

### An AI community proxy risked trust without solving engagement.

We explored an assistant trained on community leaders' posts to answer questions on their behalf. But impersonation risked authenticity, disclosure changed the expectation, and approval overhead erased the time savings—without meaningfully solving the engagement problem.

### A public skill profile depended on factors outside our control.

We explored a shareable profile of certifications, badges, and current skills to motivate return. But its value depended on employer adoption, and maintaining a public score could reward activity rather than meaningful learning, so it did not provide a compelling reason to learn.
